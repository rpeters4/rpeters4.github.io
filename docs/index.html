About

Background

Ryan is an incoming Master's student in Computer Science at Syracuse University, starting Fall 2026, alongside several years of experience running help desk operations and supervising student technology consultants at Syracuse University ITS. His academic focus is formal verification applied to modern ML systems infrastructure, an area where formal methods have barely been applied. His thesis advisor target is Prof. Endadul Hoque.

The throughline across his work is treating a formal specification as something that produces insight, not just documentation. Writing a precise spec of a distributed training protocol forces explicit questions (what is genuinely atomic, which synchronization barriers are actually required, what correctness really demands) and those questions tend to reveal optimizations.

This research builds directly on his formal-methods coursework. Temporal logic and the mu-calculus, the logics he studied for reasoning about concurrent systems, express exactly the safety & liveness properties his TLA+ specifications target, so the thesis is a continuation of that foundation rather than a departure from it.

Currently serving as Support Analyst II at Syracuse University ITS

Research

Focus Areas

⊢

Formal Methods for Distributed Training

Specifying pipeline parallelism (GPipe, 1F1B, zero-bubble), ZeRO sharding, tensor parallelism, and ring all-reduce in TLA+, with model checking and refinement mappings. The aim is to find scheduling slack, redundant synchronization, and refinement opportunities that have not been published.

∇

Specification as Discovery

A methodology built on four mechanisms: refinement-based design (deriving optimized variants from abstract correctness specs), action decomposition (splitting "atomic" actions to expose scheduling freedom), specification weakening (showing that some transformations thought unsafe are actually safe), and counterexample-guided reduction of over-cautious synchronization.

✓

Formal Verification

Model checking, safety and liveness properties, refinement mappings, and the TLA+ toolchain. Grounded in the industrial precedent for TLA+ at scale.

⇄

Distributed Systems & ML Internals

Collective communication algorithms, pipeline and tensor parallelism scheduling, and sharding protocols (FSDP, ZeRO).

∞

Adjacent Interests

Optimizer convergence formalization in Lean (the open Adam convergence question), verified tensor compilers (TASO, PET, Exo) as precedent for verification-driven optimization, and OCaml.

Pipeline Parallelism Scheduling

Visualizing how formal analysis exposes idle time (bubbles) in pipeline schedules.
